Cookie policy

Version 1.0 · Last updated 27 October 2026

openBatteryFlux sets no cookies. Not for sign-in, not for preferences, not for statistics. Sign-in uses a token kept in your browser's local storage and sent in a request header. This page lists exactly what is stored, why, and how to get rid of it.

1. Cookies: none

We set no first-party cookies, and the pages embed no third-party content — no advertising, no analytics, no social buttons, no embedded video, no external fonts or images. So there are no third-party cookies to consent to. If your browser shows a cookie from this site, the most likely source is our hosting provider's network edge, which may set strictly necessary cookies in order to deliver the site and block abuse; we cannot read or influence those. Your browser's own cookie list will show you what is there.

2. What we do keep in your browser

These are local storage and session storage entries, not cookies. None of them is readable by any other website, and none of them is sent to us with every request the way a cookie is.

That is the complete list. The tools keep your work in memory only: nothing you load or type into the simulator, comparison, calibration or validation pages is written to your browser's storage or sent to the server.

3. The consent banner

  • On your first visit a banner offers two equally prominent choices: Accept all and Essential only. Nothing is pre-selected, nothing is hidden behind a link, and closing the banner is not consent — if you do not choose, only essential storage is used until you do.
  • Essential only keeps you signed in, remembers your consent and remembers the page you were heading to. Nothing about your visit is written to our usage log.
  • Accept all adds the anonymous usage counting described above, so the project can see which cells and tools people actually use. It never includes your identity, your IP address or anything you type into a tool.
  • The cell picker's popularity total is kept apart from that usage log: the server holds a count per cell per day (times its details were opened, times it was loaded into a tool) with no identifier, no address and nothing that ties a day to a person, kept for about a year. The picker reads the all-time total to suggest the cells people use most, and the dashboard can rank the last 7 or 30 days from the same counts. It is anonymous aggregate data, so it is not linked to you, and Delete my anonymous data does not need to remove it.
  • Your choice is stored in obf_consent, and you can change it whenever you like: Cookie settings in the page footer reopens the banner with your current answer and lets you switch.
  • If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signal, we treat it as a refusal: anonymous statistics stay off unless you explicitly turn them on.

4. Removing what is stored

  • Reopen Cookie settings in the footer and choose Essential only, then press Delete my anonymous data to erase the usage rows already linked to this browser.
  • Signing out removes the session; deleting your account removes everything linked to it (see the privacy notice).
  • Clearing site data in your browser removes all of the entries above immediately. You will be signed out and the banner will ask again.

5. Changes

If what we store in your browser changes, this table changes with it and the version and date at the top are updated. Because we have no mailing list, this page is where you will see it.
