Privacy notice

Version 1.0 · Last updated 27 October 2026

openBatteryFlux is a free, open-source battery cell simulator. This notice says exactly what we store, why we store it, how long we keep it, and how you get rid of it. It is written to match what the software actually does.

1. Who is responsible

The operator of this site (the data controller) is the person or company named on the legal notice page. That page also carries our contact address for any privacy request.

2. What we collect, and why

Everything held on the server, per account and per visitor.
DataWhat it is used for
Your name and email addressTo create and sign you in to your account, and to address the two emails we send.
Your position, and the company or university you nameCollected when you create an account so we know who is using the tools: the position is required (for example student or engineer), the company or university is optional. It is never shown publicly and never shared.
Your passwordNever stored as written. We keep a PBKDF2-SHA256 hash (260,000 iterations, random salt). Nobody, including the administrator, can read your password.
Account stateRole (user or administrator), email-verified flag, and the timestamps of account creation, last login and last activity. Used to run your account page and to keep the tools locked until the address is confirmed.
Session tokenA random token that proves you are signed in, valid for 30 days. The server stores only its SHA-256 hash. The token itself lives in your own browser, and it is sent in the request header — not as a cookie.
Verification and reset linksSent by email as a one-time link; the server keeps only a hash of it. The sign-up link works for 24 hours, the password-reset link for 60 minutes.
Usage logPage visits, cells opened in the library, and cell used in the simulator, comparison, calibration or validation, plus guests sent to the sign-up wall. For a signed-in account this is linked to the account so your own profile can show it. For visitors without an account it is linked to a random identifier that we keep in your browser and store only as a 16-character hash.
Rate-limit countersWhen someone signs up, enters a wrong password or asks for a reset link, the server counts the attempts so that neither an address nor a caller can be hammered. The counter is a keyed digest of the address or e-mail address — not the value itself — and it is deleted once it has been quiet for a day.

What the usage log never contains

  • No IP address and no geolocation stored as such. The anonymous visitor identifier is a random value generated by your browser; it cannot be reversed into an address, and we never combine it with one. The one exception is the rate-limit counter in the table above: a keyed digest of the requesting address, kept for at most a day, used only to refuse repeated attempts.
  • No device fingerprint, user-agent string or advertising identifier.
  • No profile data. Measurement files you load into the calibration or validation tools are processed in your browser and never uploaded. The same is true of anything typed into a tool.
  • No payment data. No payment is processed on this site (see the refunds page).
  • No third-party analytics, advertising or social plug-ins, and no tracking across other websites. The pages load no external script, font, image or iframe at all.

Server logs kept by our hosting provider

Like every web service, the machine that serves this site writes request logs, and those logs can contain IP addresses. That is our hosting provider acting as a processor on our behalf; the logs are short-lived and are used to deliver the site and to block abuse. We do not copy the address itself into our own database: the only thing derived from it is the rate-limit digest described above, and we do not use it for anything else.

3. Why we are allowed to do this (legal bases)

  • To provide the service you asked for: your account, your session and the emails that secure it. (GDPR Article 6(1)(b), performance of a contract.)
  • Our legitimate interest: keeping the site working and reasonably safe from abuse, which is what the aggregated usage log is for. You can object to this at any time (see section 6). (Article 6(1)(f).)
  • Your consent: the anonymous usage rows created for visitors without an account. Nothing of that kind is written until you accept anonymous statistics in the banner, and we treat a browser "Global Privacy Control" or "Do Not Track" signal as a refusal. (Article 6(1)(a).)

5. Deleting your data

  • An account: open your account page and use Delete account. It is immediate and complete: the account row goes, and the sessions, the verification and reset links and the usage rows that belong to it go with it. Keep your written confirmation (there is no email confirmation, so the page is the receipt).
  • Anonymous usage data for this browser: open Cookie settings in the footer of any page and press Delete my anonymous data. That removes every usage row linked to the identifier of this browser.
  • Anything else — for example a request to see or export what we hold, or a correction: write to the address on the legal notice page. We answer within 30 days, and normally much sooner.

Deleting data also withdraws any consent tied to it. If you want the anonymous statistics to stop while the data stays, just change your choice in the cookie banner.

6. Your rights

Where the GDPR (or a similar law) applies to you, you have the right to:

  • be informed — this page is that;
  • get access to your data — your account page already shows you your profile, your tool counts and your recent activity;
  • correct it — change your display name and password on the account page;
  • erase it — the two self-service buttons in section 5;
  • restrict or object to processing based on legitimate interests — ask us, or refuse anonymous statistics in the banner;
  • portability — ask us and we will send your account data in a machine-readable format;
  • withdraw consent at any time, without it affecting what happened before;
  • complain to your national data protection authority. We would rather you told us first, so we can fix it.

Nothing in this notice limits rights you have under the law where you live.

7. Who else sees your data

  • Our hosting provider processes everything on our behalf (it runs the server, the database and the network), under a data processing agreement.
  • Resend is the email service that delivers the verification and password-reset messages. It receives your address and the message text, nothing else. It is used only when email is configured on the server.
  • Donation platforms (for example GitHub Sponsors) handle donations on their own terms. We never see your card or bank details; we may see the name you chose to make public.
  • Nobody else. We do not sell data, we do not share it for advertising, and we do not use it to train models.

Email and hosting providers may process data outside the country you live in (Resend is based in the United States). Where that happens the transfer relies on the providers' standard contractual clauses.

8. Security, and its honest limits

Passwords are salted and hashed with PBKDF2-SHA256, session and link tokens are stored only as hashes, every database query is parameterised, and the public API runs over HTTPS in production. Two limits you should know about: the public API has no rate limiting of its own (put a proxy or platform rate limit in front of it in production, as described in the deployment guide), and accounts are protected by a password alone — there is no second factor. Use a password you do not use anywhere else.

9. Cookies

We set no cookies at all. What we do store in your browser, why, and how to remove it is described in the cookie policy.

10. Age

This is a technical tool meant for people aged 16 and over (or the age of digital consent where you live). We do not knowingly collect data from children; if you believe a child has an account here, write to us and we will delete it.

11. Changes to this notice

If how we handle data changes, this page changes with it: the version number and the date at the top are updated. Because we have no mailing list, the page itself is where changes appear — check the date before relying on an old copy. Old versions are kept in the project's public repository history.

12. Contact

For any privacy question or request, use the contact address on the legal notice page. Please say what you want (access, deletion, export, a question) and which account or browser it concerns.
